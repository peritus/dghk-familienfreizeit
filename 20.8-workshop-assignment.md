# 20.8 — Workshop assignment

Module id `workshop-assignment` · factory `workshopAssignment`

## 1. Purpose and boundary

Workshop assignment gives each eligible person at most one workshop per slot, from
their rankings, under an explicit fairness objective. It owns eligibility, the
fairness ledger, under-subscription cancellation, co-assignment groups, and the
workshop section of a plan. It follows the same determinism contract as room
assignment ([solver contract](05-solver-contract.md)). People are individuals here,
not parties, which makes it structurally simpler; fairness makes the objective more
interesting.

This document is the authoritative description of workshop eligibility, fairness,
cancellation, co-assignment, and the workshop plan section.

## 2. Dependencies

Requires: [`workshop-rankings`](20.7-workshop-rankings.md), and through it
[`workshops`](20.6-workshops.md). Enhances: none.

## 3. Parameters

```ts
type WorkshopAssignmentParams = {
  objectives: readonly Objective[]        // lexicographic, most important first
  top_n: number                           // "top choices" for the primary objective
  rank_weights: readonly number[]         // weight of rank 1, rank 2, …; unranked = 0
  excluded_roles: readonly string[]       // kernel role values never assigned
  audience_rules: Readonly<Record<string, readonly string[]>>  // audience → eligible role values
  co_assignment_tag: string | null
  cancel_under_subscribed: boolean
  fill_unassigned: boolean
}

type Objective =
  | 'min-people-without-top-n'
  | 'min-people-without-any-choice'
  | 'max-rank-weight'
```

| Parameter | Default | Validation |
|---|---|---|
| `objectives` | `['min-people-without-top-n', 'min-people-without-any-choice', 'max-rank-weight']` | a permutation of the three objectives |
| `top_n` | `2` | integer ≥ 1 and ≤ `workshop-rankings.max_rank` |
| `rank_weights` | `[3, 2, 1]` | integers, non-increasing, length = `workshop-rankings.max_rank` |
| `excluded_roles` | `[]` | values of the profile's `role` vocabulary |
| `audience_rules` | `{}` | keys are values of the workshops `audience_tag`; values are role values |
| `co_assignment_tag` | `null` | fills the co-assignment role (§6) |
| `cancel_under_subscribed` | `true` | — |
| `fill_unassigned` | `true` | — |

## 7. Solver input and stages

Reads slots and workshops from `workshops`, rankings from `workshop-rankings`, and
persons with their `role` and `birthdate` labels.

### What "fair" means, decided explicitly

Fair is not a property an algorithm has by accident. It is a stated objective, in
`objectives`, that a human agreed to. Under the defaults:

1. **Primary:** minimise the number of people who receive none of their top
   `top_n` choices in any slot, across the whole occasion.
2. **Secondary:** minimise the number of people who receive none of their choices.
3. **Tertiary:** maximise total rank weight.

The order matters. Optimising total satisfaction alone gives thirty people their
first choice everywhere and eight people nothing anywhere, which scores well and is
indefensible. The primary objective caps the upside of making a happy person happier.

### The fairness ledger

```ts
type LedgerEntry = {
  satisfied_top_n: number   // slots where they got one of their top_n choices
  satisfied_any: number
  slots_seen: number        // slots in which they ranked something
}
```

The ledger is carried forward as slots are processed in order, so contention in slot
3 is resolved in favour of whoever has done worst in slots 1 and 2. Per-slot fairness
is not fairness: the same person could lose every coin flip all weekend. With the
ledger, losing early improves your odds later.

People with no ranking for a slot do not accrue ledger deficit for it: `slots_seen`
increments only when they ranked something. Otherwise not participating would be
rewarded with priority.

### Eligibility

```
eligible(person, workshop) :=
     person is not withdrawn
 and person.role not in excluded_roles
 and (workshop.audience is null
      or audience_rules has no entry for it
      or person.role in audience_rules[workshop.audience])
 and (workshop.min_age is null or age at meta.starts_on ≥ workshop.min_age)
 and (workshop.max_age is null or age at meta.starts_on ≤ workshop.max_age)
```

### Co-assignment groups

`co_assignment_tag` pairs at mutual `required` form groups by union-find. A group is
**one claimant** per slot. Its ordering of workshops is the sum of its members' ranks,
lowest first; a workshop a member did not rank counts as `max_rank + 1` for that
member. Ties break on the lowest member `person_id`. A group is eligible for a
workshop only if every member is.

> **Klettern** — group (Lena Schmidt, Tom Schmidt) assigned, combined rank 4.
> Lena ranked it 1st, Tom 3rd. Tom's own 1st choice was Töpfern.

If a group's size exceeds the remaining capacity of every workshop it could claim,
the group is split into individuals and reported, not cascaded.

### Stage `workshop-assignment.assign`

Slots in `sort_key`, then `id` order. Per slot:

```
── Step 0: constraints ───────────────────────────────────────
Apply active required custom constraints naming a workshop in this slot.
Decrement capacity; mark those people assigned. Over-capacity and
ineligible constraints are reported as conflicts, never silently resolved.

── Step 1: first choices ─────────────────────────────────────
For each workshop w in the slot, in (sort_key, id) order:
  claimants = unassigned eligible claimants whose rank 1 is w
  if claimants fit the remaining capacity: assign all
  else sort claimants by:
    1. ledger.satisfied_top_n asc    ← the fairness lever
    2. ledger.satisfied_any asc
    3. eligible workshop count asc   ← fewest alternatives
    4. lowest person_id asc          ← total order
  assign up to the remaining capacity; the rest fall through

── Step 2: cancellation sweep ────────────────────────────────
If cancel_under_subscribed: any workshop whose step-1 intake is below
min_capacity is cancelled and its people released. If anything was
cancelled, restart the slot from step 0 without those workshops.
At most one restart per slot; a second cancellation is reported, not
cascaded.

── Step 3: remaining ranks ───────────────────────────────────
Ranks 2..max_rank in order, same procedure as step 1.

── Step 4: fill ──────────────────────────────────────────────
If fill_unassigned: people still unassigned join an eligible workshop
with capacity, chosen by most remaining capacity, then sort_key, then id.
Traced as "no stated preference satisfied".

── Step 5: ledger ────────────────────────────────────────────
Record, per person, whether this slot satisfied top_n or any choice.
```

The one-restart bound keeps termination obvious and the trace readable. If it ever
fires, an organiser adjusts `min_capacity` and derives again.

Every step skips already-assigned people, so a person gets at most one workshop per
slot by construction. `derive` also asserts `(person_id, slot_id)` uniqueness, so a
refactor that breaks the invariant fails loudly in front of an organiser rather than
on Saturday morning.

## 8. Rules and scoring

No per-candidate rules. The objectives above are the scoring, and the
claimant order in step 1 is the tie-break.

## 9. Preflight checks

| Id | Severity | Fires when |

## 10. Output and diff

Plan section `workshop-assignment`:

```ts
type WorkshopAssignmentSection = {
  assignments: readonly { person_id: string, workshop_id: string, slot_id: string }[]
  cancelled: readonly { workshop_id: string, intake: number, min_capacity: number }[]
  unassigned: readonly { person_id: string, slot_id: string, reason: string }[]
  conflicts: readonly { constraint_key: string, reason: string }[]
  stats: {
    per_slot: readonly {
      slot_id: string
      assigned: number
      first_choice: number
      top_n: number
      no_preference_satisfied: number
      unassigned: number
    }[]
    overall: {
      people_without_top_n_any_slot: number   // the primary objective
      people_without_any_choice: number       // the secondary objective
      total_rank_weight: number
    }
    fill_rate: readonly { workshop_id: string, assigned: number, capacity: number }[]
  }
}
```

`people_without_top_n_any_slot` is the headline number: the objective, on the
dashboard, and what an organiser quotes when someone complains.

An `unassigned` reason names the band, not just "full":
`"no eligible workshop with capacity; 14 free places exist in ages 4–7 workshops"`.

Trace, per slot and workshop:

> **Samstag Vormittag — Töpfern** (capacity 12, ages 8+)
> 17 people ranked this first. 12 assigned, 5 to second choices.
> Assigned ahead of others on fairness: Braun Mia (no top-2 in slot 1),
> Weber Nils (no top-2 in slot 1).
> Deferred: Müller Jonas → Klettern (rank 2), Schmidt Lena → Kochen (rank 2), …

Trace, per slot summary:

> **Samstag Vormittag** — 96 assigned, 71 first choice, 89 top-two,
> 4 with no stated preference satisfied, 0 unassigned.
> **Holzwerkstatt cancelled** (intake 3, minimum 6); its 3 participants
> redistributed.

When capacity far exceeds demand, the ledger never engages; the slot summary says
"no contention" rather than showing an empty fairness section.

`diff` change kinds: `workshop-assignment-changed` per person and slot, attributed to
the person's family, and `workshop-auto-cancelled`.

## 12. Tests

- Shuffle invariance including rankings.
- The ledger favours earlier losers; people without rankings accrue no deficit.
- Cancellation restarts at most once per slot and reports a second cancellation.
- Groups claim together, order by summed rank, and split when too large.
- `(person_id, slot_id)` is unique in every plan.

## 13. When not selected

No workshop plan section exists. Workshops remain a published programme without
participants; rosters and the workshop part of the assignment page are absent.

## Workshop constraints in practice

| Situation | Belongs in |
|---|---|
| "Lena must be with her brother, she's anxious" | a `co_assignment_tag` relation |
| "This workshop needs one older kid to help" | a future per-workshop age-mix rule; until then a custom constraint |
| "Tim's rankings were entered wrong" | fix the ranking and clear any compensating constraint |
| "Klettern needs 2 free spots for late signups" | lower `capacity` on the workshop |
| "Ada would hate this even though she ranked it" | a custom constraint; a genuine judgement call |

Correcting data is preferable to a compensating constraint; the constraints screen
suggests clearing one that duplicates a corrected preference.

A workshop change is less disruptive than a room change, but rooms and workshops are
published together: one mental model is worth more here than optimality.
