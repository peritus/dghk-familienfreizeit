# 20.6 — Workshop assignment

Module id `workshop-assignment` · factory `workshopAssignment`

## 1. Purpose and boundary

Workshop assignment gives each eligible person at most one workshop per slot, from
their rankings, under an explicit stability and balanced-opportunity policy. It
owns eligibility, the previous-plan comparison, and the
workshop section of a plan. It follows the same
determinism contract as room assignment ([solver contract](05-solver-contract.md)).
People are individuals here, not parties.

This document is the authoritative description of workshop eligibility, stability,
balanced opportunity, and the workshop plan section.

## 2. Dependencies

Requires: [`workshop-rankings`](20.5-workshop-rankings.md), and through it
[`workshops`](20.4-workshops.md). Enhances: none.

## 3. Parameters

```ts
type WorkshopAssignmentParams = {
  objectives: readonly Objective[]        // lexicographic within unresolved contests
  top_n: number                           // "top choices" for the primary objective
  rank_weights: readonly number[]         // weight of rank 1, rank 2, …; unranked = 0
  fill_unassigned: boolean
}

type Objective =
  | 'min-people-without-top-n'
  | 'min-people-without-any-choice'
  | 'max-rank-weight'
```

| Parameter | Default | Validation |
|---|---|---|
| `objectives` | `['min-people-without-top-n', 'min-people-without-any-choice', 'max-rank-weight']` | a permutation of the three balanced-opportunity objectives; stability and coverage priorities are fixed |
| `top_n` | `2` | integer ≥ 1 and ≤ `workshop-rankings.max_rank` |
| `rank_weights` | `[3, 2, 1]` | integers, non-increasing, length = `workshop-rankings.max_rank` |
| `fill_unassigned` | `true` | — |

## 4. Entity kinds

None.

## 5. Events

None of its own. Cancellation is an explicit organiser event owned by the workshops
module. Organiser workshop decisions use ordinary `LabelSet` and `LabelCleared` events,
with an optional note
and the event's existing author and timestamp (§7).

## 6. Labels and tag roles

Module-owned label keys: none.


The module also consumes workshop-scoped organiser decisions through the fixed
resolver: `needs`, `provides`, `excludes`, separation, and eligibility
inputs. Existing scope and structured value schemas determine the slot and target;
free-form matching values need no custom definition. These inputs are available
only through the active module's configured controls.

## 7. Solver input and stages

Reads assigned-only slots/candidates and reserved person-slots from `workshops`,
effective rankings from `workshop-rankings`, and
persons with their `role` and `birthdate` labels, plus resolved workshop decisions
from the kernel. Notes stay in the organiser world and are not solver input.

### What "stable and fair" means, decided explicitly

Stability and fairness are not properties an algorithm has by accident. They are
explicit, explainable priorities. Under the defaults:

1. Keep a person's previous workshop when it remains valid.
2. Maximise the number of people receiving a workshop.
3. If movement is necessary, move as few people as possible.
4. In unresolved contests, prioritise people with fewer previously satisfactory
   outcomes.
5. Prefer people with fewer eligible alternatives.
6. Prefer higher-ranked choices.
7. Use person and workshop ids as final deterministic tie-breaks.

The balanced-opportunity part is measured by the existing ledger after hard
constraints, coverage, and avoidable movement have been handled:

1. **Primary:** minimise the number of people who receive none of their top
   `top_n` choices in any slot, across the whole occasion.
2. **Secondary:** minimise the number of people who receive none of their choices.
3. **Tertiary:** maximise total rank weight.

The order matters. Optimising total satisfaction alone gives thirty people their
first choice everywhere and eight people nothing anywhere, which scores well and is
indefensible. The primary objective caps the upside of making a happy person happier.

### The previous plan and movement

The previous assignment is read from the `before` plan supplied by
`deriveTransition`; it is never written as a separate domain fact. A previous
assignment is retainable only if the person, workshop, slot, eligibility,
capacity, exclusions, and separation requirements remain valid.
Retainable assignments are seeded before unresolved claims are processed.

Movement means a workshop change, an assigned-to-unassigned change, or an
unassigned-to-assigned change. A person who is new, withdrawn, or affected by a
changed hard constraint is not counted as an avoidable move. The trace states
which condition caused each change.

### The fairness ledger

```ts
type LedgerEntry = {
  satisfied_top_n: number   // slots where they got one of their top_n choices
  satisfied_any: number
  slots_seen: number        // slots in which they ranked something
}
```

The ledger is carried forward as slots are processed in order, so contention in slot
3 is resolved in favour of whoever has done worst in slots 1 and 2. Per-slot fairness
is not fairness: the same person could lose every coin flip all weekend. With the
ledger, losing early improves your odds later.

People with no effective ranking or with a sign-up reservation for a slot do not
accrue ledger deficit for it: `slots_seen`
increments only when they ranked something. Otherwise not participating would be
rewarded with priority.

### Eligibility

```
eligible(person, workshop) :=
     person is not withdrawn
 and workshop is draft assigned-mode
 and person has no active sign-up reservation in this slot
 and person.role not in workshops.excluded_roles
 and (workshop.audience is null
      or workshops.audience_rules has no entry for it
      or person.role in workshops.audience_rules[workshop.audience])
 and (workshop.min_age is null or age at meta.starts_on ≥ workshop.min_age)
 and (workshop.max_age is null or age at meta.starts_on ≤ workshop.max_age)
 and workshop is not cancelled
 and all applicable required resolver decisions allow this person and workshop
```

The last check includes free-form needs/provides matching and `excludes` targeting
this workshop. A family-scoped decision affects its people through the resolver.
Eligibility and capacity apply in every step, including explicit placement,
fill. A required decision requesting an ineligible
workshop produces a conflict; it does not bypass these checks.

### Stage `workshop-assignment.assign`

Slots in `sort_key`, then `id` order. Per slot:

```
── Step 0: retain valid previous assignments ─────────────────
Seed assignments from the `before` plan supplied by `deriveTransition` when
the person, workshop, slot, eligibility, capacity, exclusions, and separation
requirements remain valid. New or invalidated assignments are
left unresolved. These retained assignments are provisional: a feasible required
placement decision supersedes a retained assignment for the same claimant and
releases its previous capacity.

── Step 1: required placement decisions ──────────────────────
Apply positive required decisions resolving to one workshop in this slot,
in source event sequence, entity id, key, and canonical value order.
Check the whole claimant's eligibility, capacity, exclusions, and separation.
Decrement capacity and mark people assigned only for feasible decisions.
Over-capacity, ineligible, contradictory, or already-assigned-to-another-workshop
decisions are reported as conflicts, never silently resolved. A required decision
that supersedes a retained assignment is recorded as a required move.

── Step 2: first choices ─────────────────────────────────────
For each workshop w in the slot, in (sort_key, id) order:
  claimants = unassigned eligible claimants whose rank 1 is w
  if claimants fit the remaining capacity: assign all
  else sort claimants by:
    1. ledger.satisfied_top_n asc    ← balanced opportunity
    2. ledger.satisfied_any asc
    3. eligible workshop count asc   ← fewest alternatives
    4. preference rank asc
    5. lowest person_id asc          ← total order
  assign up to the remaining capacity; the rest fall through

── Step 3: remaining ranks ───────────────────────────────────
Ranks 2..max_rank in order, same procedure as step 2.

── Step 4: fill ──────────────────────────────────────────────
If fill_unassigned: people still unassigned join an eligible workshop
with capacity, chosen by most remaining capacity, then sort_key, then id.
Traced as "no stated preference satisfied".

── Step 5: ledger ────────────────────────────────────────────
Record, per person, whether this slot satisfied top_n or any choice.
```

An organiser cancellation is already present in the derived world before allocation;
it cannot be resurrected by allocation or a ranking. Exclusions and separation
continue to apply during allocation. A needs token with several
providers restricts eligible candidates; it does not force an arbitrary provider
in step 0. Preferred decisions never act as step-0 placements.

Every step skips already-assigned people, so a person gets at most one workshop per
slot by construction. `derive` also asserts `(person_id, slot_id)` uniqueness, so a
refactor that breaks the invariant fails loudly in front of an organiser rather than
on Saturday morning.

## 8. Rules and scoring

The stability, coverage, and movement priorities are fixed. Within the remaining
contests, the configured balanced-opportunity objectives keep their declared
order, followed by ranking. The fixed resolver supplies hard checks for
required decisions and configured integer soft terms for preferred decisions.
Preferred terms distinguish otherwise equal choices before the final id tie-break;
they do not override eligibility, capacity, or the stability policy. Each applied,
retained, displaced, or unsatisfied decision is explained using its source labels
and previous-plan comparison in the trace. A human-readable explanation uses
concrete reasons such as “retained: previous workshop remains valid”, “moved:
workshop capacity fell”, or “prior opportunity balance broke the tie”. The common
rule contract in
[solver contract](05-solver-contract.md) §4 applies.

## 9. Preflight checks

| Id | Severity | Fires when |
|---|---|---|

| `workshop-assignment.decision-infeasible` | error | required workshop decisions have no eligible provider or require a target they exclude |

## 10. Output and diff

Plan section `workshop-assignment` contains only solver allocation for assigned-mode
workshops. Sign-ups and interest counts remain world-level participation. Release
control consumes its per-slot results through the module's canonical scope accessor:

```ts
type WorkshopAssignmentSection = {
  assignments: readonly { person_id: string, workshop_id: string, slot_id: string }[]
  unassigned: readonly { person_id: string, slot_id: string, reason: string }[]
  conflicts: readonly { constraint_key: string, reason: string }[]
  stats: {
    per_slot: readonly {
      slot_id: string
      assigned: number
      first_choice: number
      top_n: number
      no_preference_satisfied: number
      unassigned: number
    }[]
    overall: {
      people_without_top_n_any_slot: number   // the primary objective
      people_without_any_choice: number       // the secondary objective
      total_rank_weight: number
    }
    fill_rate: readonly { workshop_id: string, assigned: number, capacity: number }[]
  }
}
```

`people_without_top_n_any_slot` is the headline number: the objective, on the
dashboard, and what an organiser quotes when someone complains.

An `unassigned` reason names the band, not just "full":
`"no eligible workshop with capacity; 14 free places exist in ages 4–7 workshops"`.

Trace, per slot and workshop:

> **Samstag Vormittag — Töpfern** (capacity 12, ages 8+)
> 17 people ranked this first. 12 assigned, 5 to second choices.
> Assigned ahead of others on fairness: Braun Mia (no top-2 in slot 1),
> Weber Nils (no top-2 in slot 1).
> Unassigned: Müller Jonas → Klettern (rank 2), Schmidt Lena → Kochen (rank 2), …

Trace, per slot summary:

> **Samstag Vormittag** — 96 assigned, 71 first choice, 89 top-two,
> 4 with no stated preference satisfied, 0 unassigned.
When capacity far exceeds demand, the ledger never engages; the slot summary says
"no contention" rather than showing an empty fairness section.

`diff` change kinds: `workshop-assignment-changed` per person and slot, attributed to
the person's family and carrying a reason; and `workshop-assignment-retained` only
when the consumer requests an explanatory
transition view. The transition compares the before and after derived plans.

## 12. Tests

- Shuffle invariance including rankings.
- An unchanged prospective event set retains every valid prior assignment.
- A local change does not reshuffle unaffected people when a feasible plan with
  fewer moves exists.
- Capacity loss or invalidation moves the minimum necessary set under the
  documented bounded-repair procedure.
- New people do not displace existing people when an eligible alternative exists.
- Every assignment change has a deterministic, human-readable reason.
- A first allocation without a previous plan follows the initial-allocation path.
- The ledger favours earlier losers; people without rankings accrue no deficit.
- Manually cancelled workshops never enter candidate arrays or allocation statistics.
- Free-form needs/provides, exclusions, separation, and eligibility
  decisions work without custom definitions, within their declared slot scopes.
- Conflicting placements never assign a person twice; required exclusions and
  separation hold in every step, including fill.
- A required target that is manually cancelled is reported as a conflict and is
  never silently resurrected.
- Preferred decisions preserve the fairness ordering; optional notes leave the
  plan hash unchanged. Cleared labels cease affecting new plans.
- `(person_id, slot_id)` is unique in every plan.
- Sign-up reservations exclude people from every placement path, including
  forced placement and fill; they add no ledger deficit or unassigned result.
- Interest and sign-up workshops never enter candidate arrays or assignment statistics.
- Fairness headline counts include only people with at least one effective ranked slot.

## 13. When not selected

No workshop plan section or allocation release scope exists. Communicated programme
details, live sign-up rosters and interest controls remain available through
`workshops`; solver rosters and assigned-result sections are absent.

## Workshop constraints in practice

| Situation | Belongs in |
|---|---|
| "This workshop needs one older kid to help" | an organiser decision for a suitable eligible person; a general age-mix rule would require module code |
| "Tim's rankings were entered wrong" | fix the ranking and clear any compensating constraint |
| "Klettern needs 2 free spots for late signups" | lower `capacity` on the workshop |
| "Ada would hate this even though she ranked it" | an organiser `excludes` label for that workshop |

Correcting data is preferable to a compensating decision. Organisers can inspect
the existing decision labels and history and explicitly clear a label they no
longer need. There is no automatic redundancy analysis or retirement workflow.

Release units, dependencies and family visibility are defined only in
[release control](06-release-control.md). The module contributes slot allocation
content and never writes communicated state itself.
